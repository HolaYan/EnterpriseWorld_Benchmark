EnterpriseWorld A realistic world for enterprise long-horizon agents

Agents can already do the steps. The question is whether they can carry each result, record, and state change into the operations that depend on it, across systems, twenty steps deep. EnterpriseWorld makes those dependencies explicit, verified, and controllable, and measures where execution breaks.

The idea

Workflows are the unit of evaluation, not tasks

In a payment workflow, a computed amount must inform the approval, the approval must authorize the payment, and reconciliation must use the transaction record that payment created. Existing benchmarks capture long, stateful tasks, but rarely make these dependencies explicit, verified, and controllable. EnterpriseWorld is built from familiar source tasks (BIRD, Spider 2.0, EnterpriseOps-Gym, WorkBench, AppWorld) woven into shared cases where every edge is a real field-level handoff that an incorrect upstream result demonstrably breaks.

01

Realistic

Enterprise operations keep their original data and judging semantics; 7 of 20 checks per workflow are inherited byte for byte from the source dataset.

02

Long-horizon

Results produced early resurface many steps later, and several must hold at once. 36% of edges span five or more steps; 24% span ten or more.

03

Structurally controllable

Matched variants deepen a required path or add prerequisites while the business setting stays fixed, so structure itself is the treatment.

04

Verifiable

Executable checks judge each operation and each dependency; replacing a correct upstream result with a wrong one must flip the downstream check.

Results

Local competence does not compose into workflow reliability

Five frontier models, one harness, one attempt per workflow. Every cell is judged by executable checks on the saved final state: a workflow counts as complete only when the case-closing operation and all of its required ancestors pass. The analysis follows four questions.

Q1

Can agents maintain correct execution across long dependency chains?

FULL WORKFLOW · LHCR-REQ (%)
Completion over the full workflow, one attempt per workflow (mean; hover for ± bootstrap std). † 50-workflow subset.

Accuracy against effort, relative to peers at each depth

Each point is one model at one evaluation depth (3, 5, 10, full; larger marker = deeper), placed relative to the five-model mean at that depth: right means more accurate than peers, up means more output tokens than peers. Subtracting the per-depth mean removes the trend that every model gets slower and less reliable with depth.
Local errors accumulate into large end-to-end reliability losses.

From depth 3 to the full workflow, completion drops by 32 to 46 points for every model; the degradation is already visible by depth 10 and continues to full completion.

More tokens and tool calls do not imply better workflow coordination.

GPT-6 Astra finishes 46.5% of workflows with 12.9k output tokens; Gemini 3.7 Flash produces 30.7k tokens and reaches 30.0%. Relative to peers at the same depth, the accurate models are also the cheaper ones.

Q2

What prevents agents from maintaining correct dependencies?

Default (plan-level)+ Procedure oracle+ Dependency-map oracle+ Handoff oraclepill = largest gain over Default
Procedure: the required procedure and rules are given upfront. Dependency map: which upstream operations constrain each step, and how. Handoff: the correct upstream object is delivered when the consumer starts its step. Hover a bar for the value and its std.
Correct intermediate results matter more than dependency maps for most models.

Handing over the correct upstream object raises completion by up to 29.5 points; exposing the dependency structure alone gives 7 to 14. Knowing which operations depend on one another is not the bottleneck; producing and carrying the result is.

Planning remains a major bottleneck for several agents, but not uniformly so.

The full procedure adds 20 to 26.5 points for four models and nothing for GPT-6 Astra (46.5 to 46.0), which instead gains most from help with dependency execution.

Q3

Can agents adapt to workflow changes?

MATCHED PERTURBATIONS · BROKEN-REQ (%)
Share of base-successful workflows that fail after the change (100 workflows, GPT-5.6 Sol, plan-level). Net completion can hide this, because some base failures also flip to success.
Successful workflow execution is fragile to controlled changes.

Across settings, 14.0 to 25.6% of previously successful workflows fail after the perturbation; deepening the path by three relays breaks the most.

Dependency changes and distractors stress agents differently.

Path deepening raises output by 0.8k to 1.8k tokens and 2 to 9 tool calls per run; distractor branches and decoy tools change effort hardly at all, yet still break 18.6% and 20.9% of successful runs.

Q4

Where does workflow coordination break down?

State-sensitive operations: observed gap vs. gap with correct prerequisites

An operation is state-sensitive when its correct target, branch, or prerequisite depends on an upstream business state (2,248 of 4,000 operations). Each dumbbell is the own-check pass rate of state-insensitive minus state-sensitive operations, in points: hollow circle scored directly, filled diamond restricted to runs whose prerequisites were correct.

Where a dependency is lost

Blocked: share of designed operations whose own check passes but whose upstream closure has already failed. The remaining columns attribute each reached dependency edge to the first failing stage: the producer's own check, the handoff (binding of the produced object), or the consumer's execution. Percent; darker is worse.

Local accuracy, chain survival, and the independent-failure baseline

How each curve is computed (plan-level condition, depth = longest dependency path from a root). Dashed, A(d): local accuracy, the share of required operations at depth d that pass their own check, over all node-runs at that depth. Solid, S(d): chain survival, the share of workflows whose required chain is still intact at depth d, meaning some required operation at depth d or deeper passes its own check and every ancestor's. Light, baseline: the survival that independent failures would produce, simulated on each workflow's real graph by letting every required operation fail independently at its observed accuracy for that depth (1,000 draws per workflow). Hover for values; pick a model to isolate it.
Local competence persists while dependency-valid progress compounds away.

At depth 12, own-check accuracy is still 59 to 81%, but only 32 to 56% of workflows keep a fully valid chain. A chain survives only if every operation on it passes, so the gap widens with depth even without cascades.

State-sensitive operations look harder only because their inputs are wrong.

Scored directly they trail state-insensitive operations by up to 10 points; once prerequisites are correct the gap is within 2 points for three models and reverses for two. 50 to 68% of failed state-sensitive operations originate in an incorrect upstream result.

Failures cluster rather than cascade beyond independence.

Observed survival runs at or slightly above the independent-failure baseline for most depths and converges with it at the deepest levels: the runs that fail tend to fail several operations at once, and most lost edges are lost at the producer (8 to 29%), not at the handoff (4 to 6%).
